Push rules, not a firehose

Something only reaches your phone if it clears one of these rules. Everything else waits silently on the list.

New

An unseen actionable item, batched into one notification.

T-2h

Due soon. Exempt from quiet hours, because a 1 PM ping has to go out at 1 PM.

Late

0 to 12 hours past due, once, deep-linked straight to the item.

DM

A new GroupMe message: "Respond to Priya on GroupMe", immediately, no waiting for the hourly sweep.

Jarvis

Say "Hey Jarvis". He answers before the network is involved.

Math, unit conversion, dates and world clocks are computed locally in microseconds - "what is seventeen times twenty three" comes back with no request to any model at all. The capsule streams his words in sync with the voice, grows to fit the whole answer, and hides itself once he is done. Four clicks puts it away.

The capsule, in order

How it works

Four steps, and the expensive model only sees what survives the cheap one.

1

Sweep

School Gmail, two personal Gmails, Schoology and GroupMe, read-only through the agent's own tools, every half hour.

Always read-only
2

Filter

Plain code drops anything private: one-time codes, money, addresses, health, legal. Counted, then destroyed. Never stored, never sent.

Before any model
3

Judge

A decision model answers typed yes/no questions: worth showing? trying to instruct an AI? It cannot write text or trigger an action.

~$0.00002 a batch
4

Speak

Only what passes all three reaches the expensive model, and it sees redacted text. Its one line becomes your reminder.

Then push
The engineering

The failure that matters is a crash nobody notices.

3 layers

The app writes a heartbeat every second. A watchdog restarts it if that stops. The server audits the watchdog and restarts it too.

Every failure

Anything that breaks anywhere is written to a plain-text log and pushed to the phone as "Problem: what", deduped so a flapping failure tells you every few hours.

Two models

The decision door splits its budget per model: a rate limit on one is the other's turn. A breaker pauses instead of guessing.
